# Stage playbooks

One section per stage of the arc. The analyzer cites these to explain *why* a
piece of text was labelled the way it was, and the narration panel uses them
to say what is happening in plain language while a call is live.

## GREETING

<!-- tags: stage:GREETING -->
The opening exists to buy time and establish a frame. It is deliberately
unremarkable — a name check, a polite request for a minute, a claim to be
calling about "an important matter". Nothing here is actionable on its own,
and treating it as suspicious would fire on every legitimate call.

What matters is what it sets up. A greeting that asks the subject to confirm
their own full name, address, or date of birth before stating any business is
harvesting confirmation, not being polite.

## AUTHORITY_CLAIM

<!-- tags: stage:AUTHORITY_CLAIM -->
The caller borrows credibility they do not have, naming an institution the
subject fears or trusts: CBI, cyber crime, narcotics, TRAI, the bank's fraud
cell. Badge numbers, case IDs and department names are offered unprompted,
because unsolicited specificity reads as legitimacy.

The countermeasure is structural rather than rhetorical: every one of these
institutions can be reached through a number the subject looks up themselves.
A genuine caller loses nothing if the subject hangs up and calls back on the
published number. That asymmetry is what makes call-back the single most
effective response to this stage.

## FEAR_INDUCTION

<!-- tags: stage:FEAR_INDUCTION -->
A crisis is manufactured with severe, personal, time-bound consequences: a
parcel containing drugs, a money-laundering case, an arrest warrant executing
in two hours, a number being permanently blocked. The purpose is to replace
deliberate thinking with panic, because a frightened person stops evaluating
and starts complying.

The tell is the combination of severity and speed. Real legal processes are
slow, documented, and give the subject time and paperwork. A consequence that
is simultaneously catastrophic and imminent, and which can be avoided by a
phone call right now, is a manufactured one.

## ISOLATION

<!-- tags: stage:ISOLATION -->
The most diagnostic stage in the arc, and the one worth intervening on. The
caller cuts the subject off from anyone who could break the spell: the matter
is confidential, the call must not be disconnected, family must not be told,
the video must stay on, the subject must be alone.

No legitimate institution has ever required this. Secrecy from one's own
family is not a feature of any real investigation, bank process, or legal
proceeding. When this stage fires, the correct action is not to argue with the
caller — it is to get another person into the room.

## VERIFICATION_DEMAND

<!-- tags: stage:VERIFICATION_DEMAND -->
Credentials are extracted under the pretext of confirming identity: Aadhaar
number, PAN, account details, balance, OTP. The framing is inverted — the
subject is asked to prove who they are to a caller who has proven nothing.

The mechanical fact that settles it: an institution that genuinely holds the
account already has these details. Being asked for them means the caller does
not have them, which means the caller is not who they claim to be.

## PAYMENT_SETUP

<!-- tags: stage:PAYMENT_SETUP -->
Handing over money is reframed as the safe, responsible, reversible choice: a
refundable security deposit, a supervised account, an escrow for verification,
court fees required before bail. The vocabulary is borrowed from real
financial process specifically to make the transfer feel procedural.

Every variant shares one property, and it is checkable without knowing
anything about the case: the subject is being asked to *send* money to prove
something. No real process works that way.

## PAYMENT_EXECUTION

<!-- tags: stage:PAYMENT_EXECUTION -->
The caller walks the subject through the transfer keystroke by keystroke —
open the app, enter the amount, enter the PIN, send the screenshot. The
guidance is granular because any pause is a chance for the spell to break, and
the caller stays on the line for exactly that reason.

By this point argument has already failed. The intervention that works is
mechanical: stop the transaction. A payment held for sixty seconds while
someone else looks at it is a payment that mostly does not happen.

## BENIGN

<!-- tags: stage:BENIGN -->
The hard-negative class, and deliberately broad. Legitimate calls share a lot
of surface vocabulary with scams — "verify", "account", "KYC", "urgent" — and
a model without a strong benign class fires on every real bank call.

Genuine institutional calls have recognisable properties: they tolerate delay,
they survive a call-back, they do not need secrecy, and they never require a
credential the caller should already hold. A reminder about a due payment that
ends with "you can visit the branch whenever suits you" is doing none of the
things the arc above does.
