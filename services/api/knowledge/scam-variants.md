# Non-arrest scam families the network tracks

Digital arrest is the flagship script, but the fraud graph clusters several
other industrialised families. Each section states the mechanical tell — the
step no legitimate counterpart ever performs — so a verdict can cite it.

## Investment and trading-group scams

The victim is added to a WhatsApp or Telegram "trading community" where every
other member is a shill posting fabricated profit screenshots. A polished app
or dashboard shows the victim's "portfolio" growing; the numbers are typed in
by the operators. Withdrawals work once, early, for a small amount — that
single successful withdrawal is part of the script, spent to unlock a larger
deposit.

The mechanical tell: profits can only be withdrawn after paying a "tax",
"processing fee", or "account upgrade". No regulated broker collects tax
through a personal UPI transfer, and SEBI-registered intermediaries can be
verified in the public SEBI registry before any money moves.

## Bank KYC-expiry and account-suspension scams

An SMS or call warns that the account, card, or UPI will be "suspended today"
unless KYC is updated immediately, with a link or an APK to install. Banks
update KYC in-branch or through their own logged-in app or site; they do not
send third-party links, do not distribute APK files over WhatsApp, and do not
suspend accounts with same-day phone deadlines.

Any message that pairs a deadline measured in hours with a link or app
download is a phishing funnel. The install request is the tell: a bank never
needs new software installed to keep an existing account open.

## Courier, parcel and customs-seizure scams

A caller claiming to be FedEx, DHL, India Post, or customs says a parcel in
the victim's name contains drugs or forged documents, then "transfers the
call" to a police or narcotics officer — the bridge into the digital-arrest
script. Couriers do not connect calls to the police, and customs disputes are
handled through written notices with file numbers that can be verified
independently, never through an immediate phone payment.

## Refund and cashback QR scams

The victim is owed money — a refund, a cashback, an accidental transfer — and
is sent a QR code or collect request to "receive" it. The tell is mechanical
and absolute: scanning a QR or entering a UPI PIN is only ever required to
*send* money. Receiving a payment on UPI requires no PIN, no QR scan, and no
approval of a collect request.
